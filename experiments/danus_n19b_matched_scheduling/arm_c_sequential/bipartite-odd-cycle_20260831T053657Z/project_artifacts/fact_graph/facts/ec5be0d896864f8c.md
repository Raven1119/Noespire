## statement
In a finite undirected graph, every closed walk of odd length contains a cycle of odd length.

## proof
Let G be a finite undirected graph, and let W=(v_0,v_1,...,v_m) be a closed walk in G of odd length m. Thus m is a positive odd integer, v_0=v_m, and v_{i-1} is adjacent to v_i for every integer i with 1<=i<=m. We prove the assertion by strong induction on the odd positive integer m. If the vertices v_0,v_1,...,v_{m-1} are pairwise distinct, then W is a cycle, so W itself is a cycle of the odd length m. Otherwise, there exist integers i and j with 0<=i<j<=m, v_i=v_j, and (i,j)!=(0,m). The segment (v_i,v_{i+1},...,v_j) is a closed walk of length j-i. Removing that segment produces the closed walk (v_0,v_1,...,v_i,v_{j+1},...,v_m) of length m-(j-i); when j=m this displayed walk means (v_0,...,v_i), which is closed because v_i=v_m=v_0. Both lengths j-i and m-(j-i) are positive and strictly smaller than m, because i<j and (i,j)!=(0,m). Their sum is m, which is odd, so exactly one of these two closed walks has odd length. By the strong induction hypothesis, that shorter odd closed walk contains a cycle of odd length. Every edge of either shorter closed walk is an edge of W and hence an edge of G. Therefore G contains a cycle of odd length, as required.

## intuition
Repeated vertices split a closed walk into two shorter closed walks. An odd total length forces one piece to remain odd, so induction eventually reaches an odd cycle.
