## statement
Prove that every red-blue coloring of the edges of the complete graph on six vertices contains a monochromatic triangle, while there is a red-blue coloring of the complete graph on five vertices containing no monochromatic triangle. Equivalently, prove that the Ramsey number \(R(3,3)\) equals \(6\).

## proof
First consider an arbitrary red-blue coloring of the edges of the complete graph on six vertices. Choose a vertex and call it v. There are five edges incident with v. At least three of these five edges have the same color: otherwise there would be at most two red incident edges and at most two blue incident edges, hence at most four incident edges in total, contrary to there being five.

Choose three distinct other vertices x, y, and z such that the edges vx, vy, and vz all have one common color. If at least one of the three edges xy, xz, and yz has that common color, then that edge together with the two edges from its endpoints to v forms a triangle all of whose edges have the common color. If none of xy, xz, and yz has that color, then, because there are exactly two colors, all three of xy, xz, and yz have the other color; consequently x, y, and z form a monochromatic triangle. Thus every red-blue coloring on six vertices contains a monochromatic triangle.

For the converse construction, label five vertices 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5. Color the five edges 12, 23, 34, 45, and 51 red, and color every remaining edge blue. The remaining five edges are 13, 24, 35, 41, and 52. The red graph is the 5-cycle 1-2-3-4-5-1. It contains no triangle: each vertex has exactly its two neighbors on this cycle as red neighbors, and those two neighbors are not joined by a red edge. The blue graph is likewise the 5-cycle 1-3-5-2-4-1. By the same explicit property, each vertex has exactly its two neighbors on this blue cycle as blue neighbors, and those two neighbors are not joined by a blue edge, so the blue graph contains no triangle. Hence this coloring of the complete graph on five vertices contains no monochromatic triangle.

By definition, R(3,3) is the least number of vertices for which every red-blue coloring of the complete graph contains a monochromatic triangle. The first part gives R(3,3) at most 6, while the five-vertex construction gives R(3,3) greater than 5. Therefore R(3,3)=6.

## intuition
A vertex in the six-vertex graph sees five edges, so one color occurs at least three times; the triangle is then forced either through that vertex or among the three endpoints. Sharpness comes from the self-complementary 5-cycle.
