## statement
Let \(p\) be a prime and let \(G\) be a finite group of order \(p^n\) for some integer \(n\ge 1\). Prove that the order of the center \(Z(G)\) is divisible by \(p\), and hence that \(Z(G)\) contains a nonidentity element.

## proof
Let \(G\) act on its underlying set by conjugation: for \(g,x\in G\), let \(g\) send \(x\) to \(gxg^{-1}\). The orbit of \(x\) is its conjugacy class \(\operatorname{Cl}_G(x)=\{gxg^{-1}:g\in G\}\). This orbit has one element if and only if \(gxg^{-1}=x\) for every \(g\in G\), equivalently if and only if \(x\in Z(G)\). Thus the singleton conjugacy classes are exactly the singleton sets belonging to the elements of \(Z(G)\).

Choose representatives \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\) for all nonsingleton conjugacy classes (with \(r=0\) allowed). For each \(i\), put \(C_G(x_i)=\{g\in G:gx_i=x_ig\}\). The map
\[
G/C_G(x_i)\longrightarrow \operatorname{Cl}_G(x_i),\qquad gC_G(x_i)\longmapsto gx_ig^{-1},
\]
is well-defined and bijective. Indeed, two elements \(g,h\in G\) have the same image precisely when \(h^{-1}g\in C_G(x_i)\), which is precisely when they determine the same left coset of \(C_G(x_i)\). Consequently
\[
|\operatorname{Cl}_G(x_i)|=[G:C_G(x_i)].
\]
Because the class of \(x_i\) is nonsingleton, \(x_i\notin Z(G)\), so \(C_G(x_i)\) is a proper subgroup of \(G\). The left cosets of \(C_G(x_i)\) partition \(G\), and each has \(|C_G(x_i)|\) elements, hence
\[
p^n=|G|=|C_G(x_i)|[G:C_G(x_i)].
\]
Since both factors are positive integers and \(p\) is prime, unique factorization implies that \(|C_G(x_i)|=p^{m_i}\) for some integer \(m_i\) with \(0\le m_i<n\). Therefore
\[
[G:C_G(x_i)]=p^{n-m_i},
\]
which is divisible by \(p\).

Partitioning \(G\) into all its conjugacy classes now gives the class equation
\[
|G|=|Z(G)|+\sum_{i=1}^{r}[G:C_G(x_i)].
\]
The left side \(|G|=p^n\) is divisible by \(p\), and every summand in the displayed sum is divisible by \(p\). It follows that \(|Z(G)|\) is divisible by \(p\). Finally, the identity element of \(G\) belongs to \(Z(G)\), so \(|Z(G)|\) is positive. A positive integer divisible by the prime \(p\) is at least \(p\ge2\). Hence \(Z(G)\) contains an element other than the identity.

## intuition
Conjugation partitions a finite p-group into central singleton orbits and noncentral orbits whose sizes are positive powers of p. Reducing the class equation modulo p leaves only the center.
