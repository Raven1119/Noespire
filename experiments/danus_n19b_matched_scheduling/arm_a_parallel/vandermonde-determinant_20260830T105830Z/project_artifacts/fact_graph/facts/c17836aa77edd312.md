## statement
Let \(n\) be a positive integer and let \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) be real numbers. Prove the Vandermonde determinant identity
\[
\det\!\begin{pmatrix}
1 & x_1 & x_1^2 & \cdots & x_1^{n-1}\\
1 & x_2 & x_2^2 & \cdots & x_2^{n-1}\\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
1 & x_n & x_n^2 & \cdots & x_n^{n-1}
\end{pmatrix}
=\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(x_j-x_i).
\]

## proof
We prove the asserted identity by induction on the positive integer \(n\).

For \(n=1\), the displayed matrix is the \(1\)-by-\(1\) matrix \((1)\), whose determinant is \(1\). The product on the right is indexed by the empty set and hence is also \(1\).

Now let \(n\ge 2\), and assume that for every choice of real numbers \(y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}\),
\[
\det\bigl(y_i^{\,j-1}\bigr)_{1\le i,j\le n-1}
=\prod_{1\le i<j\le n-1}(y_j-y_i).
\]
Fix arbitrary real numbers \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\), and denote the columns of the matrix
\[
A=\bigl(x_i^{\,j-1}\bigr)_{1\le i,j\le n}
\]
by \(C_1,\ldots,C_n\).

Beginning with \(j=n\) and proceeding downward through \(j=n-1,\ldots,2\), replace the column \(C_j\) by \(C_j-x_1C_{j-1}\). Adding a scalar multiple of one column to another column does not change a determinant, so these replacements do not change \(\det(A)\). The descending order is important: at the moment the replacement of \(C_j\) is made, only columns whose indices are greater than \(j\) have previously been changed, so the source column \(C_{j-1}\) still has its original entries.

Let \(B\) be the resulting matrix. Its first-row entry in the first column is \(1\), and for every \(j\) with \(2\le j\le n\), its first-row entry in column \(j\) is
\[
x_1^{\,j-1}-x_1x_1^{\,j-2}=0.
\]
For every \(i,j\) with \(2\le i\le n\) and \(2\le j\le n\), its entry is
\[
B_{i,j}=x_i^{\,j-1}-x_1x_i^{\,j-2}
=(x_i-x_1)x_i^{\,j-2}.
\]
Expanding \(\det(B)\) along its first row (the cofactor sign at position \((1,1)\) is \(+1\)) and then factoring \(x_i-x_1\) from the remaining row indexed by \(i\), for each \(i=2,\ldots,n\), yields
\[
\det(A)
=\det(B)
=\left(\prod_{i=2}^{n}(x_i-x_1)\right)
 \det\bigl(x_i^{\,j-2}\bigr)_{\substack{2\le i\le n\\2\le j\le n}}.
\]
After reindexing the rows and columns, the last determinant is precisely the \((n-1)\)-by-\((n-1)\) Vandermonde determinant formed from \(x_2,\ldots,x_n\). Applying the induction hypothesis with \(y_r=x_{r+1}\) for \(1\le r\le n-1\), we obtain
\[
\det\bigl(x_i^{\,j-2}\bigr)_{\substack{2\le i\le n\\2\le j\le n}}
=\prod_{2\le i<j\le n}(x_j-x_i).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\det(A)
=\left(\prod_{i=2}^{n}(x_i-x_1)\right)
 \left(\prod_{2\le i<j\le n}(x_j-x_i)\right)
=\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(x_j-x_i).
\]
This proves the identity for \(n\), and induction completes the proof for every positive integer \(n\).

## intuition
Descending column elimination makes the first row a unit vector and exposes one factor x_i-x_1 from every other row; induction supplies all remaining pairwise factors.
