## statement
Prove that every red-blue coloring of the edges of the complete graph on six vertices contains a monochromatic triangle, while there is a red-blue coloring of the complete graph on five vertices containing no monochromatic triangle. Equivalently, prove that the Ramsey number \(R(3,3)\) equals \(6\).

## proof
First consider an arbitrary red-blue coloring of all edges of the complete graph on six vertices. Fix one vertex \(v\). Exactly five edges are incident with \(v\). Since each of those five edges is either red or blue, one of the two colors occurs on at least three of them: if each color occurred on at most two, there would be at most four incident edges, contrary to the fact that there are five. Choose three distinct other endpoints \(a,b,c\) such that \(va,vb,vc\) all have this same color.

There are exactly two cases. In the first case, at least one of the three edges \(ab,bc,ca\) has the same color as \(va,vb,vc\). If, for example, \(ab\) has that color, then all three edges \(va,vb,ab\) of the triangle with vertices \(v,a,b\) have one color, so this triangle is monochromatic; the same argument applies if the edge is \(bc\) or \(ca\). In the second case, none of \(ab,bc,ca\) has that color. Because there are only two colors, all three edges \(ab,bc,ca\) then have the other color, so the triangle with vertices \(a,b,c\) is monochromatic. Thus every red-blue coloring of the complete graph on six vertices contains a monochromatic triangle.

For the coloring on five vertices, label the vertices \(0,1,2,3,4\). Color the five edges
\[
01,\ 12,\ 23,\ 34,\ 40
\]
red, and color every other edge blue. The red edges form precisely the cycle
\[
0-1-2-3-4-0.
\]
This red graph has no triangle: for any vertex of this cycle, its only two red neighbors are the preceding and following vertices in the displayed cycle, and those two vertices are not joined by a red edge.

The five edges not in the red cycle are
\[
02,\ 24,\ 41,\ 13,\ 30.
\]
Consequently the blue edges form precisely the cycle
\[
0-2-4-1-3-0.
\]
The same direct reason shows that this blue graph has no triangle: the only two blue neighbors of any vertex are its preceding and following vertices in this displayed five-cycle, and those two vertices are not joined by a blue edge. Hence this coloring of the complete graph on five vertices has neither a red triangle nor a blue triangle.

By definition, \(R(3,3)\) is the least positive integer \(n\) such that every red-blue coloring of the edges of the complete graph on \(n\) vertices contains a monochromatic triangle. The first part proves \(R(3,3)\le 6\). The exhibited coloring on five vertices proves \(R(3,3)>5\). Since \(R(3,3)\) is an integer, these two inequalities imply \(R(3,3)=6\).

## intuition
At a vertex of the six-vertex complete graph, the pigeonhole principle forces three incident edges of one color; their endpoints either supply an edge of that color or a triangle of the other color. Sharpness is witnessed because a five-cycle and its complement are both five-cycles and hence triangle-free.
