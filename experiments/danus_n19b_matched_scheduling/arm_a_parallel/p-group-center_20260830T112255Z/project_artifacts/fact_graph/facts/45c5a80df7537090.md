## statement
Let \(p\) be a prime and let \(G\) be a finite group of order \(p^n\) for some integer \(n\ge 1\). Prove that the order of the center \(Z(G)\) is divisible by \(p\), and hence that \(Z(G)\) contains a nonidentity element.

## proof
Let \(G\) act on itself by conjugation. For \(x\in G\), define
\[
C_G(x)=\{g\in G:gx=xg\}.
\]
This is a subgroup: the identity belongs to it, and if \(a,b\in C_G(x)\), then \(ab^{-1}\) also commutes with \(x\). The map
\[
gC_G(x)\longmapsto gxg^{-1}
\]
from the left cosets of \(C_G(x)\) to the conjugacy class of \(x\) is well defined and bijective. Indeed, multiplying \(g\) on the right by an element commuting with \(x\) does not change \(gxg^{-1}\); and two elements \(g_1,g_2\) produce the same conjugate precisely when \(g_2^{-1}g_1\in C_G(x)\). Therefore the conjugacy class of \(x\) has size \([G:C_G(x)]\).

The left cosets of \(C_G(x)\) partition \(G\) into equally sized sets, so
\[
|G|=[G:C_G(x)]\,|C_G(x)|.
\]
Because \(|G|=p^n\) and \(p\) is prime, both positive integer factors are powers of \(p\). Thus \([G:C_G(x)]\) is a power of \(p\). Moreover, \([G:C_G(x)]=1\) exactly when \(C_G(x)=G\), which is exactly when \(x\in Z(G)\). Consequently every conjugacy class of a noncentral element has size divisible by \(p\).

Conjugacy is an equivalence relation, so its classes partition \(G\). The class of every element of \(Z(G)\) is a singleton. Choose one representative \(x_i\) from each conjugacy class contained in \(G\setminus Z(G)\), with \(1\le i\le r\) (allowing \(r=0\)). Counting this partition gives the class equation
\[
|G|=|Z(G)|+\sum_{i=1}^{r}[G:C_G(x_i)].
\]
The left side is divisible by \(p\) because \(n\ge1\), and every term in the sum is divisible by \(p\) by the preceding paragraph. Hence \(|Z(G)|\) is divisible by \(p\).

Finally, the identity element belongs to \(Z(G)\). Since a prime \(p\) satisfies \(p\ge2\) and \(|Z(G)|\) is a positive multiple of \(p\), we have \(|Z(G)|\ge2\); therefore \(Z(G)\) contains an element different from the identity.

## intuition
The conjugation action decomposes G into central singleton classes and noncentral classes whose sizes are nontrivial powers of p. The class equation then isolates |Z(G)| modulo p.
