## statement
Let \(p\) be a prime and let \(G\) be a finite group of order \(p^n\) for some integer \(n\ge 1\). Prove that the order of the center \(Z(G)\) is divisible by \(p\), and hence that \(Z(G)\) contains a nonidentity element.

## proof
Let \(G\) act on the underlying set of \(G\) by conjugation: for \(g,x\in G\), define \(g\cdot x=gxg^{-1}\). For each \(x\in G\), its stabilizer under this action is
\[
\operatorname{Stab}_G(x)=\{g\in G:gxg^{-1}=x\}=\{g\in G:gx=xg\}=C_G(x).
\]
By the orbit-stabilizer theorem,
\[
|\operatorname{Orb}_G(x)|=[G:C_G(x)]=\frac{|G|}{|C_G(x)|}.
\]
The centralizer \(C_G(x)\) is a subgroup of \(G\). Hence Lagrange's theorem implies that \(|C_G(x)|\) divides \(|G|=p^n\). Every positive divisor of \(p^n\) is of the form \(p^m\) for an integer \(m\) with \(0\le m\le n\). Therefore
\[
|\operatorname{Orb}_G(x)|=p^{n-m}.
\]
In particular, if the orbit of \(x\) is not a singleton, then \(n-m\ge 1\), so \(p\) divides \(|\operatorname{Orb}_G(x)|\).

The orbit of \(x\) is a singleton if and only if \(gxg^{-1}=x\) for every \(g\in G\), which is equivalent to \(gx=xg\) for every \(g\in G\), which is equivalent to \(x\in Z(G)\). Thus the singleton conjugacy orbits are precisely the sets \(\{x\}\) with \(x\in Z(G)\).

Choose one representative \(x_i\) from each non-singleton conjugacy orbit, where \(1\le i\le r\); if there are no non-singleton orbits, take \(r=0\). Since the conjugacy orbits partition \(G\),
\[
|G|=|Z(G)|+\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\operatorname{Orb}_G(x_i)|.
\]
Every term in the sum is divisible by \(p\), and \(|G|=p^n\) is divisible by \(p\) because \(n\ge 1\). It follows that
\[
|Z(G)|=|G|-\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\operatorname{Orb}_G(x_i)|
\]
is divisible by \(p\).

Finally, the identity element \(e\) belongs to \(Z(G)\), so \(|Z(G)|\) is positive. Since the positive integer \(|Z(G)|\) is divisible by the prime \(p\), one has \(|Z(G)|\ge p\ge 2\). Hence \(Z(G)\) contains an element different from \(e\), that is, a nonidentity element.

## intuition
Conjugation partitions G into conjugacy classes. The classes of size one are exactly the central elements, while every other class has size a nontrivial power of p. Reducing the resulting class equation modulo p forces the center to have order divisible by p.
