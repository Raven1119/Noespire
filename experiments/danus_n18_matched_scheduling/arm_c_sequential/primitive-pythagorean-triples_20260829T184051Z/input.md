Let (x,y,z) be positive integers with

\[
x^2+y^2=z^2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\gcd(x,y,z)=1.
\]

Prove that, after possibly interchanging (x) and (y), there are coprime positive integers (m>n) of opposite parity such that

\[
x=m^2-n^2,\qquad y=2mn,\qquad z=m^2+n^2.
\]

Also prove the converse: every pair (m>n) with these properties gives a primitive Pythagorean triple by the displayed formulas.
