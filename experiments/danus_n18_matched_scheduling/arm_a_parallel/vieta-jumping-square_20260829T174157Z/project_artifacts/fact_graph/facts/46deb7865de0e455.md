## statement
Let (a) and (b) be positive integers. Suppose that

\[
\frac{a^2+b^2}{ab+1}
\]

is an integer. Prove that this integer is a perfect square.

## proof
Let
\[
k=\frac{a^2+b^2}{ab+1}.
\]
Because a and b are positive integers, both the numerator and denominator are positive, so the assumed integer k is a positive integer. Equivalently,
\[
a^2+b^2=k(ab+1). \tag{1}
\]

Assume for contradiction that k is not a perfect square. Then k is a positive integer different from 1, so k\ge 2. Keep k fixed. The original pair shows that there exists at least one pair of positive integers satisfying (1). The set of sums of the coordinates of all such pairs is therefore a nonempty set of positive integers, so it has a least element. Choose a solution whose coordinate sum is least, relabel its coordinates as a and b, and use the symmetry of (1) to arrange that a\ge b.

First, b^2>k. Indeed, rearranging (1) gives
\[
a(a-kb)=k-b^2. \tag{2}
\]
If b^2=k, then k=b^2 is a perfect square, contrary to the assumption. If b^2<k, then the right side of (2) is positive. Since a>0, the integer a-kb is then positive, hence a-kb\ge 1. Consequently a>kb\ge k because b\ge 1. It follows that
\[
a(a-kb)\ge a>k.
\]
But (2) also gives a(a-kb)=k-b^2<k because b^2>0, a contradiction. Thus b^2>k.

Define the integer
\[
c=kb-a.
\]
From (2),
\[
ac=a(kb-a)=b^2-k>0.
\]
Since a>0, this implies c>0. Also
\[
a+c=kb. \tag{3}
\]
Using ac=b^2-k and (3), compute
\[
(b-a)(b-c)
=b^2-b(a+c)+ac
=b^2-kb^2+b^2-k
=(2-k)b^2-k.
\]
Because k\ge2 and b>0, the last expression is strictly negative. Since a\ge b, equality a=b would make the left side zero, which is impossible. Hence a>b and therefore b-a<0. The product (b-a)(b-c)<0 then forces b-c>0. Thus
\[
0<c<b\le a. \tag{4}
\]

It remains to verify that (c,b) is another solution for the same k. Using c=kb-a and (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
c^2+b^2-k(cb+1)
&=(kb-a)^2+b^2-k((kb-a)b+1)\\
&=a^2+b^2-kab-k\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore c and b are positive integers satisfying
\[
c^2+b^2=k(cb+1).
\]
By (4), their coordinate sum satisfies c+b<a+b, contradicting the minimal choice of (a,b). This contradiction shows that k cannot be nonsquare. Hence the integer (a^2+b^2)/(ab+1) is a perfect square.

## intuition
Assuming a nonsquare quotient, choose a positive solution of least coordinate sum. The other Vieta root is forced to be positive and smaller than the smaller coordinate, producing an impossible descent.
