## statement
Let (ABC) be a nondegenerate triangle, and let (D,E,F) lie in the interiors of (BC,CA,AB), respectively. Prove that the cevians (AD,BE,CF) are concurrent if and only if

\[
\frac{BD}{DC}\cdot\frac{CE}{EA}\cdot\frac{AF}{FB}=1.
\]

## proof
All segment lengths in the displayed ratios are ordinary positive Euclidean lengths.

Because the triangle ABC is nondegenerate, A, B, C are affinely independent. Consequently every point P of their affine plane has unique barycentric coordinates (x,y,z), meaning real numbers x,y,z such that x+y+z=1 and P=xA+yB+zC.

Define
p=BD/DC, q=CE/EA, and r=AF/FB.
Because D,E,F lie in the interiors of BC,CA,AB, respectively, p,q,r are positive. The section-ratio definitions give the following barycentric coordinates:
D=(1/(1+p))B+(p/(1+p))C,
E=(q/(1+q))A+(1/(1+q))C,
F=(1/(1+r))A+(r/(1+r))B.
For example, the first displayed affine combination is on the segment BC; its distance from B is p/(1+p) times BC and its distance from C is 1/(1+p) times BC, so its ratio of distances from B and C is p. There is a unique point of the segment BC having that ratio, hence this combination is D. The same distance calculation on CA and AB gives the formulas for E and F.

We next characterize each cevian line. A point with barycentric coordinates (x,y,z) is on AD if and only if
z=py.                                                     (1)
Indeed, a general point of the affine line AD is (1-lambda)A+lambda D for a real number lambda. Its barycentric coordinates are
(1-lambda, lambda/(1+p), lambda p/(1+p)),
which satisfy (1). Conversely, if x+y+z=1 and z=py, put lambda=(1+p)y. Then y=lambda/(1+p), z=lambda p/(1+p), and x=1-y-z=1-lambda, so the point is on AD.

The identical explicit calculation using the displayed coordinates of E and F gives:
a point is on BE if and only if x=qz,                      (2)
and a point is on CF if and only if y=rx.                  (3)
For completeness, points of BE have barycentric coordinates
(lambda q/(1+q), 1-lambda, lambda/(1+q)),
and points of CF have barycentric coordinates
(lambda/(1+r), lambda r/(1+r), 1-lambda);
the converses follow by taking lambda=(1+q)z and lambda=(1+r)x, respectively.

Suppose first that AD,BE,CF are concurrent at a point P, and let (x,y,z) be the barycentric coordinates of P. By (1)-(3),
z=py, x=qz, and y=rx.
If y=0, then z=py=0 and x=qz=0, contradicting x+y+z=1. Hence y is nonzero. Substitution of the three equations gives
y=rx=rqz=rqpy.
Dividing by the nonzero number y yields pqr=1, which is precisely
(BD/DC)(CE/EA)(AF/FB)=1.

Conversely, suppose pqr=1. Since p,q>0, the number S=1+p+pq is positive. Define a point P by the barycentric coordinates
x=pq/S, y=1/S, z=p/S.
These coordinates sum to 1. They satisfy
z=py,
x=qz,
and y=rx because rx=rpq/S=1/S=y.
By the equivalences (1), (2), and (3), P lies on AD, BE, and CF simultaneously. Thus the three cevians are concurrent. This proves both implications.

## intuition
Positive side ratios become coefficients in barycentric line equations. The three cevian conditions form the cycle z=py, x=qz, y=rx; consistency of this cycle is exactly pqr=1.
