## statement
Let (n) be a positive integer. Prove that every sequence of (n^2+1) distinct real numbers contains either a strictly increasing subsequence of length (n+1) or a strictly decreasing subsequence of length (n+1).

## proof
Let the sequence be x_1,x_2,...,x_{n^2+1}, listed in its given order.

For every index i with 1<=i<=n^2+1, define I_i to be the largest possible length of a strictly increasing subsequence whose final term is x_i, and define D_i to be the largest possible length of a strictly decreasing subsequence whose final term is x_i. These maxima exist: the one-term subsequence consisting of x_i has length 1 and is both strictly increasing and strictly decreasing, and every subsequence ending at position i has length at most i.

Assume for contradiction that the sequence contains neither a strictly increasing subsequence of length n+1 nor a strictly decreasing subsequence of length n+1. Then every strictly increasing subsequence and every strictly decreasing subsequence has length at most n. Consequently, for every i,
1<=I_i<=n and 1<=D_i<=n.

We prove that the ordered pairs (I_i,D_i), one for each of the n^2+1 indices i, are pairwise distinct. Choose indices i<j. Since all terms of the sequence are distinct, x_i is not equal to x_j. Thus either x_i<x_j or x_i>x_j.

If x_i<x_j, take a strictly increasing subsequence of length I_i ending at x_i; such a subsequence exists by the definition of I_i. Appending x_j preserves the order of positions because i<j, and preserves strict increase because x_i<x_j. It therefore produces a strictly increasing subsequence of length I_i+1 ending at x_j. By the maximal definition of I_j, this implies I_j>=I_i+1. In particular, I_j is not equal to I_i, so (I_i,D_i) is not equal to (I_j,D_j).

If x_i>x_j, take a strictly decreasing subsequence of length D_i ending at x_i. Appending x_j preserves the order of positions because i<j, and preserves strict decrease because x_i>x_j. It produces a strictly decreasing subsequence of length D_i+1 ending at x_j. By the maximal definition of D_j, this implies D_j>=D_i+1. In particular, D_j is not equal to D_i, so (I_i,D_i) is not equal to (I_j,D_j).

Hence the n^2+1 indices give n^2+1 distinct ordered pairs (I_i,D_i). On the other hand, 1<=I_i<=n and 1<=D_i<=n, so every such ordered pair belongs to {1,2,...,n} x {1,2,...,n}. This Cartesian product has n*n=n^2 elements. It cannot contain n^2+1 distinct ordered pairs. The contradiction disproves the assumption. Therefore the sequence contains either a strictly increasing subsequence of length n+1 or a strictly decreasing subsequence of length n+1.

## intuition
Assign each position the lengths of the longest increasing and decreasing subsequences ending there. A later distinct value forces one of these two lengths to increase, so the assigned pairs are all different. If both desired long subsequences were absent, all pairs would lie in an n by n grid, which has too few points.
