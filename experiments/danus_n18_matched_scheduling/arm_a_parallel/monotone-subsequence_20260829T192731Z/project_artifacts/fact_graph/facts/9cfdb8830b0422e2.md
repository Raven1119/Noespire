## statement
Let (n) be a positive integer. Prove that every sequence of (n^2+1) distinct real numbers contains either a strictly increasing subsequence of length (n+1) or a strictly decreasing subsequence of length (n+1).

## proof
Let the given sequence be x_1,x_2,...,x_{n^2+1}, in its given order. For every index i, define p_i to be the maximum length of a strictly increasing subsequence whose last term is x_i, and define q_i to be the maximum length of a strictly decreasing subsequence whose last term is x_i. These maxima exist because the sequence is finite, and the one-term subsequence consisting of x_i shows that p_i>=1 and q_i>=1.

Assume, for contradiction, that the sequence contains neither a strictly increasing subsequence of length n+1 nor a strictly decreasing subsequence of length n+1. Then no strictly increasing or strictly decreasing subsequence has length greater than n: indeed, the first n+1 terms of any longer strictly monotone subsequence would themselves form one of length n+1. Hence, for every i,
1<=p_i<=n and 1<=q_i<=n.

We claim that the ordered pairs (p_i,q_i) are pairwise distinct. Take indices i<j. Since the terms of the original sequence are distinct real numbers, exactly one of x_i<x_j and x_i>x_j holds. If x_i<x_j, take a strictly increasing subsequence of length p_i ending at x_i and append x_j. Because i<j and x_i<x_j, this is a strictly increasing subsequence ending at x_j of length p_i+1. Therefore p_j>=p_i+1, so p_j is not equal to p_i. If x_i>x_j, the analogous operation appends x_j to a strictly decreasing subsequence of length q_i ending at x_i; it yields a strictly decreasing subsequence ending at x_j of length q_i+1. Therefore q_j>=q_i+1, so q_j is not equal to q_i. In either case (p_i,q_i) is not equal to (p_j,q_j), proving the claim.

Thus the n^2+1 indices give n^2+1 pairwise distinct ordered pairs (p_i,q_i). But every such pair belongs to {1,2,...,n} times {1,2,...,n}, a set containing exactly n^2 ordered pairs. This is impossible. The contrary assumption is false, so the original sequence contains either a strictly increasing subsequence of length n+1 or a strictly decreasing subsequence of length n+1, as required.

## intuition
Record at each position the two endpoint ranks: the longest increasing and decreasing subsequences that end there. Earlier and later positions cannot have the same rank pair, because the two corresponding values are comparable and one of the two ranks must increase. Avoiding length n+1 would leave only n^2 possible pairs for n^2+1 positions.
