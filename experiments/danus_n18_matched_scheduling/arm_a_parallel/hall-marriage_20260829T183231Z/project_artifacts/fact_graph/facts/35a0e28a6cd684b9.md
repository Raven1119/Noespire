## statement
Let (G=(X\sqcup Y,E)) be a finite bipartite graph. For (S\subseteq X), let

\[
N(S)=\{y\in Y: \text{(y) is adjacent to at least one vertex of (S)}\}.
\]

Prove that (G) has a matching saturating every vertex of (X) if and only if

\[
|N(S)|\ge |S|
\]

for every subset (S\subseteq X).

## proof
Write \(N_G(T)\) for the set of neighbors in \(Y\) of a subset \(T\subseteq X\).

First suppose that \(G\) has a matching \(M\) saturating every vertex of \(X\). For each \(x\in S\), the unique edge of \(M\) incident with \(x\) has a second endpoint \(m(x)\in Y\). Because two distinct edges of a matching have no common endpoint, the map \(x\mapsto m(x)\) is injective on \(S\). Moreover, \(m(x)\in N_G(S)\) for every \(x\in S\). Thus there is an injection from the finite set \(S\) into \(N_G(S)\), and hence \(|N_G(S)|\geq |S|\).

Conversely, suppose that \(|N_G(S)|\geq |S|\) for every \(S\subseteq X\). We prove by induction on \(n=|X|\) that \(G\) has a matching saturating \(X\).

If \(n=0\), the empty set of edges is a matching saturating \(X\). Now let \(n>0\), and assume the assertion has been proved for every finite bipartite graph whose left vertex class has cardinality strictly less than \(n\).

There are two cases.

Case 1: There is a nonempty proper subset \(A\subsetneq X\) such that \(|N_G(A)|=|A|\). Let \(G_A\) be the bipartite graph induced by the vertex classes \(A\) and \(N_G(A)\). If \(T\subseteq A\), then every neighbor in \(G\) of a vertex of \(T\) belongs to \(N_G(A)\), so the neighborhood of \(T\) in \(G_A\) is exactly \(N_G(T)\). Therefore it has cardinality at least \(|T|\). Since \(0<|A|<n\), the induction hypothesis gives a matching \(M_A\) in \(G_A\) saturating \(A\).

Let \(G_R\) be the bipartite graph induced by the vertex classes \(X\setminus A\) and \(Y\setminus N_G(A)\). For any \(B\subseteq X\setminus A\), its neighborhood in \(G_R\) is
\[
N_{G_R}(B)=N_G(B)\setminus N_G(A).
\]
Because \(A\) and \(B\) are disjoint,
\[
N_G(A\cup B)=N_G(A)\cup N_G(B)
\]
and \(|A\cup B|=|A|+|B|\). Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|N_{G_R}(B)|
&=|N_G(A\cup B)|-|N_G(A)|\\
&\geq |A\cup B|-|A|\\
&=|B|.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(G_R\) satisfies the required neighborhood inequality. Since \(A\) is nonempty, \(|X\setminus A|<n\), so the induction hypothesis gives a matching \(M_R\) in \(G_R\) saturating \(X\setminus A\). The edges in \(M_A\) have their endpoints in \(A\) and \(N_G(A)\), whereas the edges in \(M_R\) have their endpoints in \(X\setminus A\) and \(Y\setminus N_G(A)\). These four vertex sets are pairwise disjoint across the corresponding sides, so \(M_A\cup M_R\) is a matching in \(G\), and it saturates all of \(X\).

Case 2: No nonempty proper subset \(A\subsetneq X\) satisfies \(|N_G(A)|=|A|\). The assumed inequality and integrality of cardinalities then imply
\[
|N_G(A)|\geq |A|+1
\]
for every nonempty proper subset \(A\subsetneq X\). Choose a vertex \(x\in X\). Applying the assumed inequality to \(\{x\}\) shows that \(N_G(\{x\})\) is nonempty, so choose \(y\in N_G(\{x\})\). Let \(G'\) be the graph obtained by deleting \(x\) and \(y\); its bipartition is \(X'=X\setminus\{x\}\) and \(Y'=Y\setminus\{y\}\).

For \(B=\varnothing\), the neighborhood inequality in \(G'\) is immediate. If \(\varnothing\neq B\subseteq X'\), then \(B\) is a nonempty proper subset of \(X\), and deletion of \(y\) removes at most one vertex from its neighborhood. Hence
\[
|N_{G'}(B)|=|N_G(B)\setminus\{y\}|\geq |N_G(B)|-1\geq |B|.
\]
Thus \(G'\) satisfies the neighborhood inequality. Since \(|X'|=n-1\), the induction hypothesis provides a matching \(M'\) in \(G'\) saturating \(X'\). No edge of \(M'\) is incident with \(x\) or \(y\), and \(xy\in E\) by the choice of \(y\). Therefore \(M'\cup\{xy\}\) is a matching in \(G\) saturating \(X\).

In both cases such a matching exists, completing the induction and the proof of the converse. Together with the first implication, this proves the stated equivalence.

## intuition
Induct on the left side. A tight Hall set can be matched independently and removed; if no proper set is tight, every proper nonempty set has one unit of slack, so deleting the endpoints of one edge preserves Hall's inequalities.
