## statement
Let (G) be a finite connected undirected graph with at least one edge. Suppose every vertex of (G) has even degree. Prove that (G) has a closed trail that traverses every edge exactly once.

## proof
We count degree by edge-incidences, so that a loop (if loops are allowed) contributes two incidences at its vertex.

First we show that G has a nonempty closed trail. Choose a vertex s incident with an edge. Starting at s, successively append an edge that has not yet appeared in the trail and is incident with the current terminal vertex. Because G has finitely many edges, this process stops, producing a nonempty trail. Let t be its terminal vertex. If t were different from s, then the number of edge-incidences at t belonging to the trail would be odd: every passage through t contributes a paired entering and leaving incidence, while the final arrival contributes one unpaired incidence. At the moment the process stops, no edge incident with t is unused by the trail. Thus the degree of t in G would equal that odd number of used incidences, contrary to the hypothesis that every vertex has even degree. Therefore t=s, and the resulting trail is closed.

Among all closed trails in G, choose one, C, that uses as many edges as possible. Such a choice exists: the preceding paragraph gives at least one closed trail, and every trail uses at most the finite number of edges of G.

Suppose for contradiction that some edge of G is not used by C. We claim that some vertex y occurring on C is incident with an edge not used by C. Indeed, take an endpoint x of an unused edge and a path from x to any vertex occurring on C; such a path exists by connectedness. If x itself occurs on C, the chosen unused edge proves the claim. Otherwise, on the path take the first vertex y that occurs on C. The path edge by which the path first reaches y has its preceding endpoint outside the vertices occurring on C, so that edge cannot be an edge of C; it is therefore an unused edge incident with y.

Form the residual graph H by retaining all vertices of G and deleting exactly the edges used by C. Every vertex has even degree in H. To see this, at each vertex the closed trail C uses an even number of incidences: reading C cyclically, each arrival at that vertex is paired with the subsequent departure from it, and a loop contributes two incidences. Hence the residual degree is the even degree in G minus an even number.

In H, begin at y and successively append an edge not yet used in the new trail until no such extension is possible. The first edge can be chosen because y is incident with a residual edge. Finiteness again makes the process stop. The same parity argument used in the first paragraph, now applied to the even-degree graph H, shows that its terminal vertex must be its initial vertex y. We have therefore obtained a nonempty closed trail D in H based at y.

The trails C and D have no edge in common, because all edges of D lie in H. Write C with y as its initial and terminal vertex (cyclically rotating its description if necessary), traverse D from y back to y, and then traverse C from y back to y. This concatenation is a closed trail: neither constituent repeats an edge and their edge sets are disjoint. It contains every edge of C and also the nonempty set of edges of D, contradicting the maximal choice of C.

Consequently no edge of G is omitted by C. Since C is a trail, it traverses no edge more than once, and since no edge is omitted, it traverses every edge exactly once. Thus C is the required closed trail.

## intuition
Build a closed trail by following unused edges; parity prevents getting stuck anywhere except the starting vertex. If a longest closed trail omitted an edge, connectedness would locate an omitted edge at one of its vertices. Removing the trail preserves even degrees, so another closed trail can be built from that vertex and spliced in, contradicting maximality.
