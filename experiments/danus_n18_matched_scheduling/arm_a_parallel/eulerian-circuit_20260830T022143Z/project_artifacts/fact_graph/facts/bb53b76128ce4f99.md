## statement
Let (G) be a finite connected undirected graph with at least one edge. Suppose every vertex of (G) has even degree. Prove that (G) has a closed trail that traverses every edge exactly once.

## proof
Because G is finite, there is an upper bound on the number of edges in a trail. Because G has at least one edge, there is a nonempty trail. Choose a trail P having the maximum possible number of edges.

First, P is closed. Suppose instead that its initial vertex and terminal vertex are distinct. Count, with multiplicity, the incidences of edges of P at its terminal vertex. Each occurrence of that vertex at an interior position of P contributes two incidences, while its occurrence as the terminal vertex contributes one; it does not occur as the initial vertex. Thus the number of incidences there from edges of P is odd. (This count also handles loops, since a loop contributes two incidences.) The degree of the terminal vertex in G is even. Consequently the number of incidences there from edges not used by P is odd, and hence positive. Therefore some edge not used by P is incident with the terminal vertex. Appending that edge to P gives a trail with more edges than P, contradicting the choice of P. Hence the initial and terminal vertices of P coincide.

It remains to show that P uses every edge. Suppose that some edge is not used by P. There is then an unused edge incident with a vertex of P. Indeed, if the given unused edge has an endpoint on P, it itself has the required property. Otherwise, both its endpoints lie outside the vertex set of P. Since G is connected and P is nonempty, take a path from a vertex of P to one endpoint of that unused edge. Along this path, consider the first edge whose initial endpoint lies in the vertex set of P and whose other endpoint lies outside that set. This edge cannot be an edge of P, because both endpoints of every edge used by P occur on P. It is therefore an unused edge incident with a vertex of P.

Let such an unused edge be incident with a vertex x of P. Since P is closed, choose an occurrence of x and cyclically reorder the traversal of P so that it starts and ends at x. This cyclic reordering is still a trail and uses exactly the same edges as P. After completing it, traverse the unused edge incident with x. The result is a trail with one more edge than P: no edge is repeated because the appended edge was not used by P. This again contradicts the maximality of P. Thus P uses every edge of G. Since P is closed and, as a trail, traverses each of its edges exactly once, it is the required closed trail.

## intuition
A longest trail cannot stop at a different vertex because even degree leaves an unused exit. Once it is closed, any unused portion of a connected graph touches it through an unused edge, and cyclic rotation lets that edge extend the trail.
