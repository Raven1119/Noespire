## statement
Let (ABC) be a nondegenerate triangle, and let (D,E,F) lie in the interiors of (BC,CA,AB), respectively. Prove that the cevians (AD,BE,CF) are concurrent if and only if

\[
\frac{BD}{DC}\cdot\frac{CE}{EA}\cdot\frac{AF}{FB}=1.
\]

## proof
Let
\[
x=\frac{BD}{DC},\qquad y=\frac{CE}{EA},\qquad z=\frac{AF}{FB}.
\]
Because D, E, and F lie in the interiors of the indicated sides, x,y,z are positive real numbers.

Use the affine coordinate system determined by the noncollinear points A,B,C: assign A=(0,0), B=(1,0), and C=(0,1). Explicitly, a point Q with coordinates (X,Y) is the point A+X(B-A)+Y(C-A). Since B-A and C-A are linearly independent, this gives unique coordinates, and affine linear equations in X,Y describe the original straight lines. In particular, incidence and concurrence can be checked in these coordinates.

Since D is interior to BC, there is a unique real number t with 0<t<1 such that D=(1-t)B+tC. The collinear segment lengths satisfy BD=t\,BC and DC=(1-t)\,BC, so x=t/(1-t). Hence t=x/(1+x), and therefore
\[
D=\left(\frac1{1+x},\frac{x}{1+x}\right).
\]
Likewise, write E=(1-u)C+uA with 0<u<1. Then CE=u\,CA and EA=(1-u)\,CA, so y=u/(1-u). Thus u=y/(1+y) and
\[
E=\left(0,\frac1{1+y}\right).
\]
Finally, write F=(1-v)A+vB with 0<v<1. Then AF=v\,AB and FB=(1-v)\,AB, so z=v/(1-v). Thus v=z/(1+z) and
\[
F=\left(\frac z{1+z},0\right).
\]

Let (X,Y) be the coordinates of a variable point. The line AD has equation
\[
Y=xX,
\]
because A=(0,0) and D=(1/(1+x),x/(1+x)) both satisfy this equation and are distinct. The line BE has equation
\[
X+(1+y)Y=1,
\]
because B=(1,0) and E=(0,1/(1+y)) both satisfy it and are distinct. The line CF has equation
\[
(1+z)X+zY=z,
\]
because C=(0,1) and F=(z/(1+z),0) both satisfy it and are distinct.

The simultaneous equations of AD and BE give
\[
Y=xX,\qquad X+(1+y)xX=1.
\]
Set \(\Delta=1+x(1+y)=1+x+xy\). Since x,y>0, \(\Delta>0\), so AD and BE have the unique intersection
\[
P=\left(\frac1\Delta,\frac x\Delta\right).
\]
Consequently, AD, BE, and CF are concurrent if and only if P lies on CF. Substitution of P into the equation of CF gives the following chain of equivalent statements:
\[
\begin{aligned}
P\in CF
&\iff \frac{1+z}{\Delta}+\frac{zx}{\Delta}=z\\
&\iff 1+z+zx=z(1+x+xy)\\
&\iff 1+z+zx=z+zx+xyz\\
&\iff xyz=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
By the definitions of x,y,z, the equality xyz=1 is exactly
\[
\frac{BD}{DC}\cdot\frac{CE}{EA}\cdot\frac{AF}{FB}=1.
\]
Therefore the three cevians AD, BE, and CF are concurrent if and only if the displayed product of side ratios equals 1.

## intuition
Affine coordinates turn the three cevians into three linear equations. The first two determine one point, and the condition that this point satisfy the third equation simplifies exactly to the product condition.
