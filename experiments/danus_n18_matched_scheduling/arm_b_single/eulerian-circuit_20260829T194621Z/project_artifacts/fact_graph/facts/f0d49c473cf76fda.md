## statement
Let (G) be a finite connected undirected graph with at least one edge. Suppose every vertex of (G) has even degree. Prove that (G) has a closed trail that traverses every edge exactly once.

## proof
A trail uses no edge more than once. Since G has finitely many edges, every trail has length at most the number of edges of G, and since G has at least one edge there exists a trail of positive length. Hence there is a trail P having the maximum possible number of edges among all trails in G. Let a be the initial vertex of P and let b be its terminal vertex.

First, P is closed. Suppose for contradiction that a and b are distinct. Consider the incidences at b of the edges used by P, where a loop at b contributes two incidences, as it does to the degree. Every passage of P through b before its terminal occurrence contributes two incidences, one from the edge by which P arrives and one from the edge by which P departs. The terminal occurrence contributes one additional incidence from the final arriving edge. There is no initial unpaired incidence at b because a is not b. Therefore the number of incidences at b belonging to edges of P is odd. The degree of b in G is even, so the number of incidences at b belonging to edges not used by P is the difference of an even integer and an odd integer; it is therefore a positive odd integer. In particular, some edge incident with b is not used by P. Appending that unused edge to P gives a trail with more edges than P, because the appended edge was not previously used. This contradicts the maximal choice of P. Thus a=b, so P is closed.

It remains to prove that P uses every edge. Suppose for contradiction that an edge h of G is not used by P, and choose an endpoint x of h. If x is a vertex occurring on P, then h itself is an unused edge incident with a vertex of P. If x is not a vertex occurring on P, connectedness of G gives a path from x to some vertex occurring on P. Choose such a path with the fewest edges. Let q be its last edge and let w be the endpoint of q that occurs on P. The other endpoint of q does not occur on P, since otherwise deleting q from the chosen path would give a shorter path from x to a vertex occurring on P. Consequently q is not an edge used by P, because both endpoints of every edge used by P occur on P. Thus, in either case, there is an edge q not used by P that is incident with a vertex w occurring on P.

Because P is closed, choose an occurrence of w in its vertex sequence and cyclically rotate the sequence of its edges and vertices at that occurrence. The rotated sequence is again a closed trail, uses exactly the same edges as P, and has initial and terminal vertex w. Append q to this rotated trail. The result is a trail with one more edge than P: all old edges remain distinct and q is not among them. This again contradicts the maximal choice of P. Hence P omits no edge of G.

The trail P is closed, it uses every edge of G, and by the definition of a trail it uses no edge more than once. Therefore P traverses every edge exactly once.

## intuition
Choose a longest trail. Even degree forces its two endpoints to coincide. If the resulting closed trail omitted an edge, connectedness would place an unused edge next to some trail vertex; rotating the closed trail to start there and appending that edge would make it longer.
