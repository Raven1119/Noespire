## statement
Characterize the nonnegative integers representable in the form
\[
a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc
\]
with \(a,b,c\) nonnegative integers. Prove that a nonnegative integer \(N\) has such a representation if and only if either \(3\nmid N\) or \(9\mid N\).

## proof
Define
\[
F=a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc,\qquad
S=a+b+c,\qquad
Q=a^2+b^2+c^2-ab-bc-ca.
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\[
F=SQ. \tag{1}
\]
We will also use the identities
\[
Q=S^2-3(ab+bc+ca) \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
2Q=(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2. \tag{3}
\]

First suppose that \(N=F\) for some nonnegative integers \(a,b,c\), and suppose that \(3\mid N\). For every integer \(x\), the integer \(x^3-x=x(x-1)(x+1)\) is divisible by \(3\), because one of three consecutive integers is divisible by \(3\). Hence \(x^3\equiv x\pmod 3\). It follows that
\[
N=F\equiv a+b+c=S\pmod 3,
\]
because \(3abc\equiv0\pmod3\). Thus \(3\mid S\). Equation (2) then shows that \(3\mid Q\): the term \(S^2\) is divisible by \(3\), and the term \(3(ab+bc+ca)\) is divisible by \(3\). By (1), both factors \(S\) and \(Q\) are divisible by \(3\), so \(9\mid F=N\). Therefore any represented \(N\) satisfies \(3\nmid N\) or \(9\mid N\).

Conversely, let \(N\) be a nonnegative integer satisfying \(3\nmid N\) or \(9\mid N\).

If \(3\nmid N\), the division algorithm gives exactly one of the two forms \(N=3t+1\) or \(N=3t+2\), where \(t\) is a nonnegative integer.

If \(N=3t+1\), choose
\[
(a,b,c)=(t+1,t,t).
\]
All three entries are nonnegative, their sum is \(S=3t+1=N\), and (3) gives
\[
2Q=1^2+0^2+(-1)^2=2,
\]
so \(Q=1\). Equation (1) gives \(F=SQ=N\).

If \(N=3t+2\), choose
\[
(a,b,c)=(t+1,t+1,t).
\]
Again all entries are nonnegative, their sum is \(S=3t+2=N\), and (3) gives
\[
2Q=0^2+1^2+(-1)^2=2,
\]
so \(Q=1\). Thus (1) again gives \(F=N\).

It remains to treat \(9\mid N\). Write \(N=9k\), where \(k\) is a nonnegative integer. If \(k=0\), take \((a,b,c)=(0,0,0)\), which gives \(F=0=N\). If \(k\ge1\), take
\[
(a,b,c)=(k+1,k,k-1).
\]
These entries are nonnegative. Their sum is \(S=3k\), and (3) gives
\[
2Q=1^2+1^2+(-2)^2=6,
\]
so \(Q=3\). By (1),
\[
F=SQ=(3k)(3)=9k=N.
\]
Thus every nonnegative \(N\) with \(3\nmid N\) or \(9\mid N\) is represented, completing both directions.

## intuition
The cubic factors into the sum of the variables and a nonnegative quadratic difference factor. Divisibility by 3 forces both factors to be divisible by 3, while choosing triples with quadratic factor 1 or 3 constructs every allowed value.
