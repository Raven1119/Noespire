## statement
For which real polynomials \(p\) is there a real polynomial \(q\) such that

\[
p(p(x))-x=(p(x)-x)^2q(x)
\]

for all real \(x\)?

## proof
The answer is
\[
p(x)=x+c\quad\text{or}\quad p(x)=-x+c,
\]
where \(c\) is any real constant.

First suppose that real polynomials \(p,q\) satisfy the displayed identity. Define
\[
f(x)=p(x)-x.
\]
Then \(p(y)=y+f(y)\) for every real \(y\), and hence
\[
p(p(x))-x=p(x)+f(p(x))-x=f(x)+f(x+f(x)).
\]
Therefore
\[
f(x)+f(x+f(x))=f(x)^2q(x). \tag{1}
\]

If \(f\) is the zero polynomial, then \(p(x)=x\), which is in the first claimed family. Assume from now on that \(f\) is nonzero, and let \(n=\deg f\). The finite Taylor identity for the polynomial \(f\) is exact:
\[
f(x+f(x))=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(x)}{k!}f(x)^k.
\]
Consequently there is a real polynomial
\[
H(x)=\sum_{k=2}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(x)}{k!}f(x)^{k-2}
\]
(with the sum interpreted as zero when \(n<2\)) such that
\[
f(x)+f(x+f(x))=f(x)(2+f'(x))+f(x)^2H(x). \tag{2}
\]
Combining (1) and (2) gives
\[
f(x)(2+f'(x))=f(x)^2(q(x)-H(x)).
\]
Since \(f\ne0\) and \(\mathbb R[x]\) is an integral domain, cancellation yields
\[
2+f'(x)=f(x)(q(x)-H(x)). \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(f\) divides \(2+f'\).

If \(f\) is constant, then \(p(x)=x+c\) for a real constant \(c\). If \(f\) is nonconstant and has degree \(n\ge1\), then \(2+f'\) is either the zero polynomial or has degree at most \(n-1\). Equation (3) rules out the latter possibility, because a nonzero multiple of the degree-\(n\) polynomial \(f\) has degree at least \(n\). Hence
\[
2+f'(x)=0.
\]
It follows that \(f(x)=-2x+c\) for a real constant \(c\), and therefore \(p(x)=-x+c\). This proves necessity.

Conversely, if \(p(x)=x+c\), then \(p(p(x))-x=2c\) and \(p(x)-x=c\). For \(c\ne0\), the identity holds with the constant polynomial \(q(x)=2/c\); for \(c=0\), it holds with \(q(x)=0\). If \(p(x)=-x+c\), then \(p(p(x))=x\), so the identity holds with \(q(x)=0\). Thus every polynomial in the two claimed families works, completing the classification.

## intuition
Subtract the identity map. Modulo the square of f=p-x, composition by x+f retains only the first-order Taylor term, forcing the lower-degree polynomial 2+f' to be divisible by f.
